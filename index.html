1. Reference Editions & Translation Notes

Referenced against the 1971 Random House First Edition (751 pp., ISBN 0-394-46200-9, abbreviated RH) and the 1972 Fawcett Crest Paperback (768 pp., abbreviated FC). Spanish editions (Hijos de Torremolinos, Plaza & Janés) and French editions (Les Déracinés) occasionally soften 1960s American drug vernacular ("skin-popping", "Big-H", "kif", "acid") or translate colonial administrative titles in Vwarda and Portuguese Mozambique more generically.

2. Source-Supported Details vs. Artistic Choices

Every panel strictly separates Book-Supported Canon (ages, nationalities, physical builds, hair, injuries, key garments, and settings) from Period-Appropriate Artistic Choices (1968–1970 garment colors, specific weave patterns, and 35mm Kodachrome lighting) where the text leaves minor visual details unspecified.

3. Period Realism & Visual Continuity

Designed as an authentic late-1960s / 1970 Kodachrome and Kodak Portra visual companion to Michener's novel, preserving the work's unvarnished adult tone, sun-drenched Mediterranean and African settings, Pamplona bullring drama, 1968 political unrest, and the tragic Marrakech arc.
